# Frontier Classroom Design Proposal

## North Star

Make the platform feel like a live clinical skills bay, not a case generator with panels around it. A student should always understand three things: what patient they are facing, what action they can take next, and how the patient changed because of that action.

## 1. Case Selection UX

Replace the current broad case picker with a clinical mission board.

- Primary paths: Practice a skill, run a full case, join classroom, continue previous.
- Filters students understand: presentation, acuity, year level, core skill, equipment focus, time limit.
- Case cards should show: presenting complaint, first impression, required competencies, expected equipment, estimated time, and difficulty.
- Educator mode should allow a case playlist: warm-up, main case, escalation variant, debrief.
- Random generation should become "smart random": match level, avoid repeating the same category, and surface why it chose the case.

## 2. Treatment Flow

The treatment bay should behave like a real patient interaction loop.

- Open a jump bag, choose an item, prepare/apply it, watch it appear on the mannequin, then reassess.
- Every treatment should produce one of four responses: improvement, no change, deterioration, or patient refusal/contraindication.
- The monitor should be an evidence source, not the answer key: students must connect, measure, and reassess.
- Device overlays must stay anatomy-aware: masks fit the face, IVs sit on limbs, pads avoid covering exam hotspots, ventilation shows chest response.
- Inappropriate actions should trigger realistic pushback: gagging with OPA in an awake patient, refusal if conscious, warning if oxygen/treatment choice is wrong for the context.

## 3. Classroom Experience

The classroom should feel like an instructor-led simulation room.

- Instructor view: control tower with case state, vitals, timeline, injects, expected actions, and rubric.
- Student view: focused patient bay, no hidden scoring clutter, clear role in the team.
- Team mode: assign airway, circulation, medication, lead clinician, scribe.
- Instructor injects: deterioration, bystander update, hospital radio, equipment failure, patient refusal, new finding.
- Debrief: synchronized timeline of assessment, treatments, vitals, audio events, scoring, and teachable moments.

## 4. Rollout Plan

- Phase 1: Case-selection mission board with better filters and case cards.
- Phase 2: Treatment action loop: prepare, apply, visible patient state, reassess.
- Phase 3: Classroom control tower and team roles.
- Phase 4: Debrief replay with timeline, vitals, PDF export, and resource links.

## Quality Bar

- Students should never hunt through UI to find a basic clinical action.
- No floating panel should block the anatomy it is asking the student to examine.
- Motion should signal state, not distract from readings.
- Every major action should leave evidence on the patient, the monitor, the timeline, or the debrief.
